## 2. Details of the key findings

### A1 OU stationary variance (fixed, default off)

Model: `dη = -(1/ζ)η dt + sqrt(2/ζ) dW`, continuous stationary variance = 1.
Euler-Maruyama (the original implementation, identical to the C++) gives the
discrete stationary variance

```
v_E = 1 / (1 - dt/(2ζ))          # ζ=1, dt=0.25 -> 1.143 (+14.3%)
```

Probe P1 (20k chains × 3000 steps): Euler **1.125 (+12.5%)**, exact update
**0.984 (−1.6%)**. Thus, whenever `sigma` is not small (the paper sweeps σ up
to 1.0), the extrinsic-noise amplitude is systematically overestimated. The
exact one-step transition (Gillespie 1996) is

```
η' = η e^{-dt/ζ} + sqrt(1 - e^{-2dt/ζ}) N(0,1)
```

whose stationary variance is exactly 1 for any dt, and which does not blow up
for dt>2ζ. The **default `ou_exact=False` keeps the validated baseline
unchanged**; set it to `True` for quantitative noise studies.

### A2 dt-convergence measurement (2026-10-22, open item #4)

`batch.dt_convergence_table(cfg, n_seeds=8, t_short=400)` runs the same
configuration at `dt`, `dt/2`, `dt/4` (8 seeds each) and reports the seed-mean
per observable, successive differences `e_k` and the observed order
`p = log2(e_0/e_1)`. **Caveat (by construction):** the differences upper-bound
the *total* dt-sensitivity of the coupled system — Lie splitting of the
field–cell coupling **plus** explicit-Euler GRN, Euler–Maruyama OU, event rates
and the explicitly dt-dependent mechanics relaxation (`relax` is a per-step
fraction). Isolating the Lie-splitting term alone would need a Strang variant
of the simulator (still open).

Measured (2026-10-22, this machine; `t_short=400 h`, 8 seeds):

| config | observable | mean(dt) | mean(dt/2) | mean(dt/4) | e₀ (rel) | e₁ | order p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| legacy default (no field) | total | 1970.8 | 2002.1 | 2043.5 | 31.3 (1.6%) | 41.4 | −0.40 |
| | sc | 127.6 | 126.4 | 133.0 | 1.2 (0.9%) | 6.6 | −2.46 |
| | ta1 | 911.2 | 949.9 | 929.5 | 38.7 (4.1%) | 20.4 | +0.93 |
| | ta2 | 931.9 | 925.8 | 981.0 | 6.2 (0.7%) | 55.2 | −3.16 |
| spatial + paracrine (`example_3d_spatial` cfg) | total | 414.1 | 376.6 | 407.1 | 37.5 (**10.0%**) | 30.5 | +0.30 |
| | sc | 55.7 | 46.0 | 56.1 | 9.7 (**21.1%**) | 10.1 | −0.06 |
| | ta1 | 177.4 | 165.0 | 162.8 | 12.4 (7.5%) | 2.2 | +2.49 |
| | ta2 | 180.9 | 165.5 | 188.1 | 15.5 (9.3%) | 22.6 | −0.55 |

Reading (honest):
1. **Legacy (validated) baseline:** the dt-halving shifts are 0.7–4.1% relative
   and **below the 8-seed Monte-Carlo standard error** (e.g. total: e₀=31 vs
   se≈92) — the discretisation error at dt=0.25 is *not resolvable* at this
   seed count, consistent with the baseline being dt-robust at the validated
   operating point. No action needed for the paper's Table-3-scale claims.
2. **Spatial mode:** the first-halving shifts are **10–21%** relative — an
   order of magnitude larger — because the field–cell coupling and the
   dt-dependent mechanics relaxation enter here. **But `e₁` does not shrink**
   (it is ≥ e₀ for three of four observables), so the observed order is *not*
   ≈1 and the first-order nature of the scheme **cannot be demonstrated** with
   8 seeds: the MC noise (se ≈ 5–18) is comparable to e₀. Practical
   consequence: spatial-mode results at dt=0.25 should be quoted with an
   **O(10%) dt-sensitivity** caveat, or re-run with variance reduction /
   more seeds if a convergence order must be shown.
3. The measurement is a *total*-error bound (see caveat above); it does not
   by itself indict the Lie splitting — it says the coupled spatial system is
   dt-sensitive at the 10% level and that the sensitivity is not cleanly
   first-order at feasible seed counts.

### B1 Two-moment matching ≠ distribution matching

cpg(moment) marks each site with probability `φ(v)`; with `M=m0+1` the mean and
variance match beta exactly (probe: 0.1107/0.1107, 0.00161/0.00161). The
**tails differ**: at v=0.1, `P(u<0.05)` is 0.040 for beta and 0.083 for cpg
(**2.1×**), KS=0.034. The near-zero probability directly controls mark loss /
dedifferentiation, so a cross-kernel comparison **must not report only the
first two moments**; report tail probabilities or a full distribution distance.
(Relevant literature: Huh & Paulsson 2011 show that partitioning/noise
mechanisms are "remarkably difficult to separate" from the first two moments.)

### B3/B4 Multigene degeneration: exact for G=2, fails for G≥3

- `S=∏H+(x_i)`, `K=∏H+(x_thresh)=0.5^G`. Box rule (all x_i>0.5) vs
  single-threshold product rule mismatch: **G=2 6.3%, G=3 12.8%, G=4 19.1%**
  (probe P3).
- `regulation="product"` coincides with additive for G=2; for G=3 with full
  mutual repression, product (AND) → single-gene-high steady state
  (0.079,0.079,1.701), additive (OR) → two-gene-high steady state
  (0.086,1.709,1.709), `max|diff|=1.63` (probe P4).
  **Conclusion: for G>2 one must choose AND(product) or OR(additive)
  explicitly; the default cannot be carried over.**

### C2 Nearest-grid sampling (fixed, default off)

Probe P5 (h=4, decay length 10 μm, smooth field): nearest-node sampling has a
mean relative error of **27.3%** and a maximum of 0.361. Trilinear sampling +
conservative trilinear source deposition reduce the mean relative error to
**2.03%** (**13.4×** improvement), and the eight weights sum to exactly 1
(mass conservation). Default `field_interp="nearest"` keeps the baseline;
`"linear"` is recommended for quantitative M4 work.

### C1 Cartesian grid vs spherical domain (implemented, default off)

The reflecting boundary is applied on the six cube faces, but cells are
confined to a sphere of radius `R_dom`. Grid corners (|r|>R_dom) contain no
cells yet can accumulate field, i.e. the effective domain is larger than the
cell domain. When the decay length `sqrt(D/k) << R_dom` this is negligible;
otherwise the field should be zeroed / zero-flux outside the sphere.
**Implemented** as `space.sphere_mask=True` (Dirichlet at r=R_dom).

### B2 Sister correlation (implemented, default off)

`beta/gaussian/cpg` draw the two daughters **independently** given `φ(v)`, so
the sister correlation is 0 (probe `corr(d1,d2)≈-0.006`). Real sisters share
the mother's marks and should be positively correlated. M8 (bet-hedging) uses
"lineage autocorrelation scaling with γ" as its discriminant; independent
sisters artificially suppress that correlation and may **underestimate the M8
signal**. **Implemented** as `EpiConfig.sister_corr=ρ`:
- beta: Gaussian copula (latent correlation ρ, exact Beta marginals);
- cpg(moment): correlated per-site uniforms;
- gaussian: raises a clear error rather than silently returning independent
  sisters.

### 8.1 OU variance vs `zeta` (dt=0.25)

| zeta | 0.25 | 0.5 | 1.0 | 2.0 | 4.0 |
